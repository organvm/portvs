## Axioms

The Beacon is not a command tower. It is a lawful emission that helps an adrift
entity discover whether it has a viable path toward Home.

| Term | System primitive | Operational counterpart |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adrift | A token whose relations, rules, or governance loci are unresolved. | A repo, config, prompt, plan, or agent state with no trusted path back to purpose, owner, or next action. |
| Home | A converged locus where formal, operational, and governance layers agree. | A verified directory, repo, registry row, issue, or artifact that can safely receive the work. |
| Vacuum | A named absence where convergence should exist but does not. | A missing anchor, broken path, stale claim, untracked artifact, unresolved registry gap, or unsupported assumption. |
| Telos | A rule-governed orientation toward intended completion. | The accepted purpose, closeout condition, or next-mode decision that guides what should happen next. |
| Beacon | A high-density emission of orientation without compulsion. | A protocol, document, status signal, CLI output, or rendered artifact that makes the next truthful path easier to perceive. |
| Portal | A translation surface between minds, organs, or substrates. | A layer that lets formal, aesthetic, operational, and governance readers all access the same core signal. |
| Tether | A reversible relation that preserves return-path context. | A link, note, path, provenance marker, branch, issue, or handoff that can be detached or revised without damage. |
| Return | A movement from drift toward verified Home, without claiming completion early. | A checked path back to the right directory, repo, registry, or plan state, with uncertainty and next gates preserved. |

Metaphysical claim: a lighthouse guides the lost.
Operational counterpart: a Beacon emits enough verified structure for an adrift
entity to identify a possible Home, while preserving consent, uncertainty, and
reversibility.

Metaphysical claim: Home is not merely a place, but a recognitional event.
Operational counterpart: Home requires evidence that the formal name, physical
artifact, and governance claim refer to the same thing.

Metaphysical claim: Vacuum is sacred absence, not failure.
Operational counterpart: a vacuum is logged as a gap to resolve, not silently
filled with invented certainty.

## Emission Model

Semantic density is the amount of orientation carried per unit of signal. A
Beacon with high semantic density names the object, its current state, the
governing law, the evidence path, and the next gate without forcing the receiver
to reconstruct the whole system.

Pulse/oscillation is the repeatable rhythm by which the Beacon re-emits state.
In a tool this may be a heartbeat, status line, recurring audit, or watch loop.
In a document it is a repeated structure: claim, anchor, evidence, uncertainty,
next mode.

Empathy gradient is the protocol's sensitivity to receiver distance. A formal
mind may need axioms and falsification gates. An aesthetic mind may need image,
rhythm, and felt contrast. An operational mind may need paths and commands. A
governance mind may need authorization, ownership, and closeout criteria.

Resonance modes define how the Beacon meets the adrift entity:

- Recognition mode: "this is about me" becomes possible through precise naming.
- Orientation mode: the next truthful direction becomes visible.
- Translation mode: the same signal becomes legible across Theoria, Poiesis,
  operations, and governance.
- Return mode: the receiver gains a reversible path toward Home.
- Refusal mode: the Beacon declines to guide when consent, evidence, or authority
  is missing.

## Portal Model

The Portal keeps the Beacon from collapsing into a single audience. Each layer
receives the same Telos through its own interface.

| Layer | Reader | Beacon form | Failure to avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Formal | Theoria | Definition, axiom, falsification criterion, source law. | Poetic claims with no testable structure. |
| Aesthetic | Poiesis | Image, rhythm, sensory contrast, symbolic continuity. | Sterile correctness that cannot attract attention or care. |
| Operational | Builder/operator | Paths, commands, file state, verification steps. | Inspiration that cannot be acted on. |
| Governance | Steward/reviewer | Consent gates, scope boundaries, ownership, durability rules. | Guidance that becomes coercion, overreach, or false completion. |

The Portal rule: no layer may contradict another. If the aesthetic layer says
"Home," the operational layer must show what Home is. If the formal layer says
"Vacuum," the governance layer must preserve the uncertainty instead of closing
it cosmetically.

## Authorization Gates

The Beacon may guide only when these gates hold:

- Consent: the receiver or governing context permits guidance.
- Non-coercion: the Beacon may orient, recommend, or refuse, but must not force a
  path by hiding alternatives or consequences.
- Uncertainty disclosure: every unverified anchor, stale memory, inferred Home,
  or missing path must be named as such.
- No false-home claim: a local file, attractive metaphor, or partial match must
  not be called Home until formal, operational, and governance evidence agree.
- Reversible tethering: links, branches, notes, and handoffs must preserve a way
  back or a way out.
- Scope fidelity: the Beacon must not promote a seed into a registry, repo, or
  public artifact without explicit authority.
- Vacuum preservation: absence must remain visible until resolved; the Beacon
  must not fill silence with invented certainty.
- Closeout honesty: Return can be partial. A truthful "still embryonic" is
  better than a premature completion claim.
